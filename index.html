How UTMs work & Luminate conventions

UTM parameters are tags added to the end of a link that tell Google Analytics where a visit came from. Without them, clicks from LinkedIn, Instagram link-in-bio, newsletters and partners often land in GA4 as “Referral”, “(not set)”, or untagged sources like ig — which is exactly why our weekly social number under-counts real traffic. A tagged link fixes attribution at the source.

A finished link looks like:
https://luminatedata.com/reports/x?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=release-strategies-2026

ParameterWhat it answersLuminate examples
utm_source *Which platform?linkedin, instagram, x, reddit, bluesky, newsletter, email
utm_medium *What kind of channel?social, paid-social, email, newsletter, referral, cpc
utm_campaign *Which initiative?release-strategies-2026, midyear-report-2026, data-forum-2026
utm_contentWhich variant/placement?link-in-bio, cta-button, post-20260903, carousel
utm_termWhich paid keyword?music industry data (paid search only)

House rules: always lowercase, use hyphens not spaces, keep a single value per concept (don't mix x and twitter), and tag every outbound link — social posts, link-in-bio, newsletters, partner placements. Consistency is what makes the data add up.
